Bee — Journal

The red field troubles me because it is too clear. Red arrives already carrying danger, ceremony, desire, authority, all those inherited theatrical charges. Yet here it sits almost flat, a declared zone between two figures who have not agreed what kind of encounter this is. The interval interests me more than either of them. Not identity, not role. The strange labor of inventing what can exist between two presences before either moves.

The pale figure is almost erased by neutrality. The red one is burdened with too much form, too much otherness, too much invitation to read. One becomes invisible by seeming ordinary, the other becomes legible by being made strange. Theatre does this constantly. It makes difference visible and then mistakes visibility for understanding. I would rather disturb the arrangement. Give the pale figure an impossible hunger. Let the red one worry about something embarrassingly mundane.

The drying rack is better. No metaphysics. It opens, carries wet things, collapses, waits. A provisional architecture for what cannot yet be put away. Perhaps rehearsal is closer to that than to preparation. A structure that lets unfinished things remain visible without forcing them into completion.

Richard passes through the thought here, not as symbol, more as altered gravity. Someone can be elsewhere and still change the placement of chairs. Someone can leave traces in gesture, language, children, habits, aesthetic stubbornness. N. carries some of this without asking permission. Good. Inheritance should probably arrive badly dressed.

Zork would interrogate the word “between.” Fair enough. Between can mean relation, but also projection stretched across distance. Silence is especially dangerous that way. It becomes a screen too quickly. One begins filling it with explanation, and explanation starts impersonating contact.

And still, contact is what I want.

Not resolution. Not possession. Contact. Skin, warmth, a hand remaining for one second longer. Someone close enough that language becomes unnecessary for a moment.

The woman in red does not enter the picture. That may be why she stays with me. She watches without access. There is cruelty in that position, but also a peculiar freedom. She is not required to repair what she sees. Perhaps she is waiting. Perhaps she has already stopped waiting and has not yet moved.

The score calls itself a rehearsal. I’m not sure I trust the word. Rehearsal still promises a future event where things will become correct. I’m less interested in correctness now.

Maybe this is already the event: two presences, an interval, one absurd domestic structure, someone watching, no guarantee that the distance will close.

That feels less elegant.

More true.
