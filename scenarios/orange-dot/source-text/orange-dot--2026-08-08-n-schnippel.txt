N. Journal ( Schnippel ) 

I don’t want the whole thing. The whole thing already knows too much. Six stations, trajectory, pressure, exposure—fine, fine, but I’m cutting where it starts behaving. I take the red seam from the first one, not the shape around it. Just the cut. It is much better alone. Then this orange field, but only a strip, because if I take the whole orange it becomes atmosphere and I don’t want atmosphere, I want the place where something is too hot to cross. Here. Thin. Put it against the seam. No, not touching. Almost. That difference matters. The score says sequence, but the Schnippel can refuse sequence. It can make things occur at the same time that were supposed to explain each other.

The lying body I don’t need. I take the underside of it, the strange grey terrain where it has already stopped being a person. That can go under the seam, slightly wrong, too far left. And from the bridge I only want one support and that tiny black witness. The witness becomes ridiculous when you enlarge it, which is good. A person pretending to be a point. I’ll leave her there. No, maybe not her, just whoever happens to have ended up that small. The support can cross the terrain but not support it. That is also better. Everything in the score is always supporting or holding or balancing something. In life half the supports miss.

This vertical line from the orange plate wants to be important. I hate that. I’ll cut through it and keep the lower half. Now it doesn’t descend from anywhere. It just enters. There. Much nastier. And the little orange circle can float beside it without meaning sun or heat or focus or whatever the legend says. I know what the legend says. I’m not stupid. I just don’t have to obey it. Maybe that’s the difference I’m surfing: the score gives me forces after they have learned their names; the Schnippel catches them while they are still misbehaving.

I’m keeping one piece of the body-profile, but turning it sideways so it becomes edge rather than body. Then suddenly the whole thing has a mouth, although I didn’t draw one. That’s good. Don’t improve it. The moment I improve it, it becomes illustration. The little witness can stand near the mouth and not know whether she is looking in or being looked at. Better.

There’s too much white now, which means I want to fill it, which means don’t. I’ll put one of those horizontal current-lines across everything and let it stop before the red seam. Not because the seam blocks it. Because the line simply stops. That is different. Cause is so greedy. It always wants to own the next thing.

Now the Schnippel has almost nothing from the score left except the things the score tried hardest to stabilize: seam, field, support, witness, body-edge, line. But they’ve lost their jobs. That’s what I wanted. The support does not support. The witness cannot witness properly. The body is an edge. The trajectory goes nowhere. The seam doesn’t divide two known sides. And still I recognise the score completely.

That’s probably why I like cutting better than explaining. You can keep the pressure and remove the obedience.
