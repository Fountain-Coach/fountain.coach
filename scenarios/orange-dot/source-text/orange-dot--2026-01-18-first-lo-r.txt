First LO - R.

It starts clean. Not romance, not story. Just a contact event. A door, a room, bodies in proximity. Acting-school atmosphere: everyone fluent in contact, everyone trained in signals, and yet integration is low. The field is bright with competence. You can do closeness like a technique. You can also lose it like a technique.

R. is there as a real person, not an allegory. A colleague with a nervous system. A face, a voice, a timing. I don’t need to explain her. I only need to admit what happened in me: contact went live. Orange heart: contact = 1. Warmth present. The world briefly makes sense in the body.

Then the cut.

Not gradual. Not negotiated. A sentence lands like a stamp on the moment — not an argument, not a moral lecture, more like an alarm from her system. The body keeps moving for a fraction of a second because bodies lag behind meaning. The mind halts. A cut line draws itself through the room. And the field changes: from lived event to observed event. I become witness to myself while still standing inside the scene.

That’s the glitch: closeness achieved, but safety revoked.

And then the engine boots.

This is where the “art” is born as a survival function, not a choice. The patch is writing, but not writing-as-literature. Writing-as-traction. Breath compresses. Syntax compresses. The sentences shorten because the lungs shorten. A physiological style.

Not like a poem.
Like a brace.

The first lines aren’t meant to be read. They are meant to prevent collapse. They are shock markers, then rhythm, then ritual. They become a small machine I can hold when I can’t hold the contact.

Something happened.
It cut.
Too fast.
Why.
My fault.
No.
Still cut.
Still gone.
Hold it.
Here.
With words.

One line.
Next line.
Don’t stop.

Each short sentence is a hand on the radiator: weak heat, but heat. Not enough to warm the whole room, enough to stop freezing. This is the key: the patch doesn’t restore love, it restores function. It keeps me upright. It keeps the contact event from turning into a blank.

And because it works, the system learns it.

The mind takes what was unrepeatable — a moment with R. — and turns it into a repeatable artifact. Replayable. Editable. “Improvement” becomes a promise. The promise becomes a loop. The loop becomes the real object of attention. R. is not erased, but she becomes the trigger, while the loop becomes the location.

That’s the cold trick: the warm person is outside the box, but the box is inside me.

Later, an authority figure tries to interrupt the compilation. Burn it. Throw it into the river. Delete the patch. The advice has logic: don’t let the coping become the self. But the system is loyal to what saved it. So the command doesn’t end the engine; it merely adds a new module: shame about the engine.

Which is why this “First LO” can feel cold when described like engineering. Because the engineering is what happens when warmth is threatened. The engineering is a defensive form of tenderness: an attempt to keep the ember alive without having to ask anyone to hold it.

So if I’m precise, the first LO is not “R. as fantasy.” It’s R. as ignition, and the loop as the thing that returns. The person triggers; the compiler re-runs. And the short sentences are the compiled output: breath turned into syntax turned into a replay artifact.

And still — R. matters. Not as a character I need to explain, but as the first time my body learned this exact lesson: when contact cuts too fast, I don’t just grieve. I build a machine. I call it meaning. I call it writing. But it begins as a reflex: a way to keep a small fire in winter air.
