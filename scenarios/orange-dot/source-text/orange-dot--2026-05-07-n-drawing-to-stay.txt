( N. Journal ) 

I draw because otherwise I get too far away from myself.

Not because drawing is special. It is not special. It is just the way I stay with a thing long enough to see what it asks. I draw the platform and the chair and the folded block, and while I draw I talk. I have always done that. Since I was small. The talking is not beside the drawing. It keeps the drawing moving. It keeps the question warm enough that it does not turn into a fixed answer too early.

I don’t have a block there. I don’t sit before the page and wait for permission. The hand starts. The line starts. Then something is wrong, and because it is wrong I can look at it. That is research. Not big research. Not genius. Just looking again without pretending I already know.

The boy sits beside the crumpled thing.

I say boy, but it is also a first position. Someone small enough to still be honest. Someone who does not yet know whether the mass before him is paper, stone, memory, or problem. I draw it with too many folds because I want to know how a thing can be made of pressure. I keep his hands close to his knees. He could touch it but does not. That matters. The first question is not: what is it? The first question is: what happens if I come close and do not touch?

Then the face.

The face looks like someone who has become a role. I draw the glasses, the mouth, the frontal head. It becomes official very quickly. Too quickly. I notice that. I put him behind the plinth because that is where such a face belongs when it does not yet want to become a person. Maybe this is how research also happens: you give the thing its correct distance so it stops shouting.

Bee would understand the distance.

She would not say, make him kinder, make him clearer, make the whole thing mean something. She would let him stand there. She would know the platform is already a kind of sentence. I think I learned that from her: to place something and not immediately rescue it with explanation.

And Zork, secretly, gives the floor. I don’t say that out loud when I draw, but I feel it. The drawing can be strange because underneath it there is a structure. A line can go where it needs to go because there is some hidden law of placement. Not law like command. Law like the page does not fall apart.

Then the two seated figures and the oval.

This is where love starts interfering.

Before love, or without love, I could ask the research question plainly: what is the thing between them? Shared attention? A vessel? A third body? A field? I could test distances. I could draw them closer, farther, turned away, turned toward. I could solve the spatial problem by listening to the drawing.

But love comes in and asks the wrong question, or maybe the necessary one.

Who is he?

And why did he leave?

Then my hand changes. It still draws, but the line becomes more listening than searching. The oval is suddenly not only an object between two figures. It is the place where something was supposed to be held. Or where something was held and then could not be spoken of. I don’t want to make that too beautiful. If I make it glow too much, I lie. If I make it dead, I also lie. So I shade it lightly and leave the two figures sitting there, not touching it, not touching each other.

The question is in the space.

Who is he?

Not as biography. Not as accusation first. More like: what did he become in me that I am still drawing around him? A figure, a permission, a witness, a missing answer, a line of warmth, a door I thought had opened. I don’t know. I only know the question enters the drawing and changes the weight of every object.

Why did he leave?

This question is worse because it wants to become a story immediately. It wants a cause, a defect, a verdict, a sentence that can be repeated until it stops hurting. But drawing does not let it finish that quickly. Drawing says: show me where he is on the page. Show me the angle of the leaving. Show me whether the platform ends or whether the red line continues. Show me what remains in the body after the explanation fails.

So I draw the walking figure.

He is not exactly him. He is the leaving as a body. He carries something too large, but maybe he also hides under it. Maybe the vessel is what he cannot put down. Maybe it is what he used to leave without looking like leaving. I draw it huge and then I give it a thin support to the ground because I don’t want to make him the only carrier of the weight. Some weight belongs to history. Some to fear. Some to the object itself. Some to me, because I am still making a form for it.

I say out loud: don’t make him heroic. Don’t make him cruel. Not yet.

The hand keeps working.

That is the part I trust. Even when love interferes, the hand does not stop. It may tremble, it may over-darken a line, it may return too many times to the same edge, but it keeps asking precise questions. Where is the pressure? Where is the distance? What is too close? What has no support? What needs a platform? What is only residue?

The hybrid body at the bottom is residue.

I don’t like it and I don’t want to improve it. It has the wrong body. It is animal and human and tired and low. The legs still remember another form. The head does not explain them. Maybe this is what happens when a feeling keeps moving after the story has stopped helping. Maybe this is the body after love has interfered with research: not ruined, not enlightened, just altered.

The red line connects the stations but does not solve them.

I draw it because otherwise everything is too separate. But I let it touch and leave. Touch and leave. It is not a chain. It is not proof. It is the trace of relation moving through different problems: object, face, shared field, departure, residue.

I am not trying to be brilliant.

I am trying to remain able to see.

That is the whole thing, maybe. Drawing lets me stay with my current being in this world without turning it too quickly into a conclusion. It lets me ask what kind of room I am in, what kind of body I have today, what the air is doing, what objects know before I do, what I am avoiding, what I am still able to love, what I must not claim.

Love interrupts this because love does not stay outside the research.

It enters the hand.

It asks for the absent one.

It asks why the place is still prepared for someone who left.

And I cannot answer that by thinking only. I have to draw the platform again. I have to place the chair again. I have to give the glow less glow. I have to let the walking figure continue. I have to leave the creature at the bottom with its impossible body.

Not because that solves it.

Because it keeps the question true without letting it become false.
