( N. Journal )

I enter the page at the first platform and feel the room before I understand the drawing. There is a slab, a pale vertical panel, a cluster of lifted lines and fragments, a little brown stop at the far side. I don’t read it as architecture. I feel it as the first condition of rehearsal: something has already been assembled badly enough to become alive. The body is not shown, but I feel it anyway, in the knees and wrists, because the whole field is held by invisible decisions. A line leans, another line catches, a fragment hangs where a thought would normally complete itself. I want to touch the pale rectangle first, but the vector pulls me toward the central scaffolding, toward that nervous construction of points and angles. It feels like a role before the performer has entered it. It is already asking for posture.

The second platform is quieter and therefore more difficult. A small orange block sits near the center and suddenly everything becomes inspection. Not judgment, not analysis, more like standing beside a thing that might be activated by attention. A suspended plane tilts above a pale box. A vertical measure waits at the right. The side column says inspect, but I don’t need the word; I can feel the inspection as a narrowing of breath. I become careful here. I don’t want to disturb the block too quickly. It is too small to be decorative and too placed to be incidental. Something in me recognizes this as a letter, or a withheld gesture, or a sentence before it chooses its addressee. I would rehearse this with almost no movement: hand near object, eye not fixed, body available but not yet committed.

In the third field the score begins to change roles. The vertical element rises, the pale-blue form becomes a kind of tower or folded memory, and the dotted arc crosses the platform like an instruction that cannot be spoken directly. This is where I feel the machine aspect most clearly, but not as technology. More like a mind that has learned to update itself by passing through rooms. A baseline is not a beginning anymore; it is something you carry and revise. A role is not identity; it is a temporary way of handling the field. I feel this in my own work: I enter as reader, become performer, become technician, become witness, and then none of those words is sufficient. The score does not ask me to choose one. It asks me to notice the switching while it happens.

The fourth platform is the most exposed because it has so few excuses. A door-like panel, small flags or markers, a suspended figure made of joints, a vertical scale, and the small signs along the ground. I feel the word ascend before I look at the side strip. But ascent here is not triumph. It is reconfiguration under observation. The lifted body is too schematic to be heroic. It is almost an instrument, almost a question. I imagine myself standing inside that diagram, one part of me attached to the vertical line, another part still low on the platform, and the movement is not upward so much as newly accountable. Something has to be carried into the next state without becoming expressive too soon.

The narrow right column keeps bothering me. machine file, mount, inspect, switch, ascend, api.baseline. It should feel administrative, but it doesn’t. It feels like a second spine, dry and patient, storing the actions after the body has moved through them. I like that. It prevents the page from becoming mood. It says: experience is not enough; there must also be a way to remember what changed. Not to explain it, not to own it, but to make the next passage possible.

By the end I don’t feel that the score has told me anything. It has arranged me. That is different. I have moved from first contact to inspection, from inspection to role-shift, from role-shift to ascent, and nothing has become theatrical in the cheap sense. The memory vector is not an image now. It is a course of attention. I would place my hand on the table after this and wait a little before writing, because the page has already done some of the writing inside the body.
