# A Sound Must Earn Its Place

*On acoustic craft, listening, and what no render can decide.*

**Status:** Revised editorial proof responding to the writer's reading and audio-illustration review. Unpublished and not yet author-approved.

In *Kino After Dark*, the illuminated façade of Berlin's Kino International gave us a place to begin. I wanted the residue of cinema: a room with some life left in it after the film, a little distance, a phrase that might have belonged to someone who was no longer there.

[KINO-ILLUSTRATION:architectural-plate]

The first patch did what it was asked to do. A fixed bass pattern held its place. Noise became percussion and air. A repeating sample supplied movement. MIDI 2.0 carried control; Ear measured the output and changed an echo. The timing was correct. The connections were real.

But I did not hear that room. I heard a demonstration: something papery and toyish where the sound should have carried weight. The patch had proved that its parts could talk to one another, not that there was anything worth hearing between them.

The obvious escape was to go native. An AUv3 instrument would be at home in a professional audio host, with better scheduling, more familiar controls and fewer excuses about the browser. Those advantages matter. But changing the host would not make a thin bass line substantial or give a borrowed phrase the life of a performance. I was asking a question of the sound and receiving an answer about the software.

We have met the same difference in a simpler material. In *The Door Is a Score*, a door closing was not merely an audio file waiting to be triggered. Someone closes it; someone else may hear it. The distance matters, as do the hand, the surface, the force, the hesitation. A careful closing can be more disturbing than a violent one. What we hear is an event, not the name of a sample.

Synthetic sound deserves that degree of attention. Its source may be recorded, modeled, performed or built from oscillators. The choice is not a ranking of technologies. It is the beginning of a material inquiry: how does this thing start, what changes while it sounds, and what is left in the air when it stops? A small noise can take hours of work before it has the right weight. Some imperfections have to be removed; others are exactly what makes the sound believable.

[KINO-ILLUSTRATION:night-photograph]

We made two original Csound patches for the same short score: air, a low resonance, two metallic gestures and their disappearance. Both are sixteen seconds long, without a borrowed recording or a jazz sample. In the first, each event is simply triggered and left almost bare, with a uniform envelope and no convincing room. In the second, the same events acquire differing attacks, unstable partials, a slowly changing perspective and a reverberant tail. Their overall listening levels have been matched. The second is a direction to audition, not a finished production.

Hear the two attempts at the same scene. The score does not change. The material and the imagined room do.

The comparison does not turn the judgment over to measurements. Those can tell us that the second version occupies the stereo field differently, contains different transients and has a measurable room response. Only listening can tell us whether these changes make the imagined place more credible. I would rather keep the imperfect first take beside the second than erase the question that led from one to the other.

This is why our audio standard cannot end at successful rendering. Technical acceptance asks whether the instrument ran as specified and whether its result can be traced and reproduced. Acoustic acceptance asks what happened to the signal: its material quality, noise, dynamics, perspective and suitability for the medium. Artistic acceptance happens when somebody listens in the intended context and decides whether the sound belongs.

Those judgments need not agree. An intentionally damaged recording may be right for the scene. A perfectly clean one may be wrong. The standard is not a requirement to make everything beautiful. It is a way of refusing to let a limitation pass unnoticed as an aesthetic decision.

Csound Teatro, MIDI 2.0, Ear, Sing, Satzfunk and Reframe can help us keep the source, the gesture, the processing and the different takes in relation. They can preserve what we tried, what changed and why we kept a version. None can relieve the people making the work of listening to it.

An atmosphere after a film may need very little: distant air, a low resonance, a phrase that comes once and does not return. That little is difficult to make convincing. Its silences must be listened to as carefully as its sounds. When it finally belongs, the apparatus recedes.

**A sound is not ready because the machine can make it. It is ready when the work can answer for it.**
