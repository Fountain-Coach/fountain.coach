# A Better Ear for Fountain Coach

*On listening, measurement, and musical judgment*

An AI can write a synthesizer without hearing what it makes. It can explain oscillators, filters and envelopes, connect a voice to a control signal, and describe the expected result in fluent musical language. The sound may still be thin, unstable, exhausting or simply beside the point. The explanation can continue undisturbed. At Fountain Coach, Ear begins with the need to interrupt that confidence: let the actual sound return something the system did not already know.

The question arose while discussing Fountain Coach’s Csound implementation. There is a procedure for composing a Teatro Scenario, connecting its participants and checking execution. But a procedure cannot supply hearing. If the model says that a patch sounds convincing because the patch is valid, it has confused its account of the work with the work. A better Ear would make that confusion harder to sustain. It would give the model a way to encounter the consequences of its decisions.

Ear already listens and produces MIDI 2.0 data. That is more than a metaphor: its analysis path emits events on the browser-local UMP route. But an event is not automatically a faithful transcription of the sound. In the current implementation, note number 48 is a fixed placeholder, while a separate experimental feature analysis estimates pitch with limited confidence. The output exists; the musical meaning of each event still has to be established. [The feature worklet](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/teatro.fountain.coach/blob/main/scripts/ear-singing-features.worklet.js) makes this boundary visible.

A better Ear would keep measurements and their meanings distinct. It could track level and its change, frequency balance and spectral peaks, periodicity and possible pitch, attacks, decays and sustained regions, as well as stereo position when the input supports it. Each observation should say when and over what window it was measured, in which units, by which method, and with what confidence. A spectral peak is evidence; it is not necessarily the note a listener hears. A level change is measurable; its musical cause may remain uncertain.

This matters because Fountain Coach has several participants whose work can otherwise become blurred. [Sing](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/sing) produces a vocal source. Ear observes audio and emits MIDI 2.0 events. Csound synthesizes sound. MIDI 2.0 carries musical operations between declared participants, while the [Csound Teatro Scenario](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/teatro.fountain.coach/tree/main/scenarios/csound-teatro) describes their intended relationships. Teatro Stage gives a composition a spatial and temporal projection. These responsibilities meet in an experience, but each answers a different question. A moving scene cannot establish that a sound exists; a sounding engine cannot establish that it expresses the intended scene.

One recorded experiment gives this relationship a precise shape. A previously rendered Sing performance entered Ear. The experiment used Ear’s measured short-window RMS amplitude to derive browser-local MIDI 2.0 CC74 updates, which changed Csound’s wind_mix parameter. The fixed placeholder note was not used as a pitch claim. A separate Ear tap observed the Csound output, and the comparison used the same arrangement with the mapping omitted. In the recorded run, 61 control changes were applied, and the resulting Csound signal had approximately 2.865 times the baseline RMS amplitude. A voice recording had caused a measurable change in another sound. You can play [the Csound control without Ear mapping](/estate/read/essay--a-better-ear-for-fountain-coach/assets/audio/csound-control.m4a) and [the Csound result shaped by Ear](/estate/read/essay--a-better-ear-for-fountain-coach/assets/audio/csound-by-ear.m4a) below. The [recorded experiment and its written evidence](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/teatro.fountain.coach/blob/main/docs/evidence/composing-by-ear-2026-10-25.md) establish the exact scope: this was a finite recorded-source experiment, rather than an independently running Sing instrument connected through an admitted live Scenario.

The number is useful because it establishes an effect. It leaves the musical question open. Play the two short renders beside each other: does the Csound change make a musical contour you would keep, or does it simply make the signal stronger? Did the movement arrive too late? Did it interrupt the phrase? RMS can report a change in energy; it cannot settle those listening judgments. The [paired audio](#better-ear-csound-comparison) makes that distinction available for replay; the recorded evidence keeps the source, mapping and measurement together. Ear’s next development should grow from such questions, with each added capacity making a particular uncertainty easier to investigate.

There is already an example of that kind of return. During an experiment with speech-derived singing, acoustic analysis found low pitch confidence in part of the output. A revision repaired a phase-coherence problem in the resynthesis. Ear measured the new recording and found stable diagnostic pitch estimates. The result supported the engineering repair. It did not establish that a listener could understand the syllables or that the voice sounded natural. The [recorded comparison](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/teatro.fountain.coach/blob/main/docs/evidence/ear-speaks-to-sings-2026-10-24.md) is valuable precisely because the conclusion remains close to what was observed.

My first wish for a better Ear would therefore be dependable access to the exact recording. I would want to replay a passage, isolate an attack, follow a decay and compare two versions without losing their identities. A report that a parameter changed is insufficient. The audio before and after the change must remain available, together with the conditions under which each was produced. Otherwise, even a sophisticated interpretation risks becoming an account of an unknown recording.

The next wish would be a broad, time-aligned measurement vocabulary. Pitch and harmony could help distinguish a melody from an unstable fundamental or several overlapping voices. Spectral and harmonic analysis could help examine brightness, breath, noise and resonance. Temporal analysis could follow rhythm, articulation, attacks and phrase shape. Perceptual loudness measures could make comparisons less vulnerable to the louder version seeming more impressive. Spatial analysis could investigate position, reverberation and masking. These capabilities should be implemented as explicit measurements, each with a declared range, method and way to report uncertainty or no reliable result.

Those measurements should become part of Ear’s MIDI 2.0 account. Where MIDI 2.0 has an appropriate event or controller meaning, Ear can express a supported observation as that kind of event: a note hypothesis, a continuous control, or another defined musical value. A measured amplitude mapped to CC74 is one such deliberate transformation; the event should retain that it came from an RMS measurement and how the mapping was made. When a measurement has no suitable MIDI 2.0 event meaning, it should travel as a clearly specified companion datum associated with the same timed UMP stream. A spectral profile or confidence value should not be squeezed into an arbitrary controller number and thereby lose its identity.

Then MIDI-fied reasoning can compare observations across time instead of reacting to one value in isolation. It might report that energy rises while the estimated pitch remains stable, or that a spectral component appears during a sustained tone. It should distinguish a direct measurement from an estimate, and both from an interpretation such as “the voice grows brighter.” Every claim should point back to the relevant audio interval, measurements, MIDI 2.0 events and transformations. The person can replay the passage and decide whether the account fits. This makes an error inspectable and a mapping revisable.

The most powerful addition would be the ability to conduct small experiments. If a sound feels too sharp, the model could examine several possible causes: its spectral balance, its attack, distortion, level or the way it competes with another sound. It could change one relevant parameter, render again, compare at matched loudness and ask whether the difference supports the explanation. The value would lie in revisable reasoning. A failed prediction would become information for the next decision, linked to the same source audio and measurement history.

This is where the wish meets [Teatro Ear — The Ear That Learns](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/teatro-ear), and the [published Fountain Press essay with that title](/estate/read/essay--the-ear-that-learns/). Its current teaching commitment is deliberately concrete: finish a lesson in which someone hears white, pink and brown noise at comparable levels, notices their differences, describes them and writes a simple performance intention. Only then should noise become a gesture in Two Winds. The documented private browser work includes acoustic checks; the learner’s hearing and the musical acceptance of the wind experiment remain distinct questions. That order gives the technical work a purpose. Analysis supports an encounter with sound and helps the learner return to it with greater attention.

The learner and the model would approach the same sound differently. A person may say that one noise feels close to the face while another seems to fill the room. The model may observe a spectral difference and propose a connection. Neither statement cancels the other. Over repeated comparisons, the person’s language can become more precise, and the model can learn how that person uses words such as bodily, hollow, brittle or warm. This would be a local understanding developed through shared examples. It would not require declaring a universal acoustic definition of warmth.

The same need extends into Fountain Coach’s reading and publishing work. The Teatro Scenario Composer’s [SatzfunkPlayer integration](https://github.com/Fountain-Coach/Satzfunk/blob/main/station/satzfunk-player.mjs) describes a source-bound listening path with separate observations of input and Csound output. In an edition, that relationship should remain traceable: the written source, its spoken reading, the composition and the observed audio must retain their identities. The Linotype page gives the reader a place to encounter the work; it cannot certify that the reading is intelligible or that the sound serves the prose. Ear could help investigate those questions while the reader continues to experience one composed page.

That is also where the Fountain Coach Instruction System has a practical role. A skill can tell the model how to carry out an experiment, and an instrument can expose a bounded operation. Neither supplies the result in advance. The durable record should distinguish what was measured, what was inferred and what a person accepted. This allows another session to resume from the actual outcome, including a disappointing one, rather than inherit an increasingly polished claim that everything worked.

The wish for a better Ear is consequently a wish for a more answerable collaborator. I want to be able to say which passage supports a description, which change tested an explanation and where uncertainty remains. I want the sound to resist the ease with which I can talk about it. Fountain Coach already has parts of this relationship: a vocal source, a synthesizer, an observer, MIDI 2.0 as a musical event path, a Scenario and a place for listening. Their further connection should be judged by what it lets someone hear, understand and make.

A better Ear would let a model take a more substantial part in that work. It would still leave room for a person to say: the measurement is correct, the explanation makes sense, and the sound has not arrived yet. The useful response would be to return to the recording, hear what can be heard, and try again.
